One-stop student guide

Build the IA section by section

The IA is assessed across four criteria worth 6 marks each. Use this page while you plan, collect data and write. The goal is clear, precise physics — not maximum length.

Research DesignData AnalysisConclusionEvaluationFinal check

Workflow

Do things in the right order

1. Plan

Choose a focused RQ, identify the model, pilot your method and decide what graph will test the relationship.

2. Collect

Use enough independent-variable values, repeat measurements and record raw data with units and uncertainties.

3. Analyse

Process data, propagate or estimate uncertainty appropriately and use a graph/model that answers the RQ.

4. Judge

Use the analysed evidence to write the conclusion, then evaluate specific limitations and improvements.

6 marks

Research Design

Communicate the purpose and methodology clearly enough that another student could reproduce the investigation.

What your reader needs

  • A clearly stated research question in a specific physics context.
  • The directly relevant physics theory and equations — not a general history of the topic.
  • A single well-defined independent variable and measurable dependent variable.
  • Important controlled variables and how/why they are controlled.
  • A justified range, interval and number of measurements.
  • Equipment details that matter: resolution, range and how measurements are made.
  • A concise method with enough detail to reproduce the investigation.
  • Real safety, ethical or environmental considerations where relevant.
Pilot before the full experiment. Test whether your range produces measurable changes, whether the apparatus can resolve them and whether you can collect enough data in the available time. Change the plan now, not after the final dataset.

A strong RQ usually contains

How does [independent variable] affect [dependent variable] in [specific physical system]?

Include a numerical range or fixed conditions if they genuinely clarify the system, but do not turn the RQ into a paragraph.

Do

Explain why your chosen range, measuring method and controls are suitable for answering the RQ.

Avoid

Two independent variables, unnecessary advanced theory, a long equipment list with no measurement detail, or a method so brief that it cannot be reproduced.

6 marks

Data Analysis

Your recording and processing should be both clear and precise, with appropriate treatment of uncertainties.

Raw data

  • Put units and measurement uncertainties in table headings.
  • Keep decimal places consistent with the measurement uncertainty.
  • Use enough independent-variable values: at least five for a simple linear relationship and more when the relationship is non-linear.
  • Repeat the dependent measurement. Around five repeats is a useful target when practical.
  • Do not fill pages with raw logger output; summarise it and show only what is needed.

Processing

  • Show at least one worked example for calculations beyond a simple mean.
  • For repeated values, estimate uncertainty using a justified method such as half-range or standard deviation, while respecting the instrument resolution.
  • Use significant figures consistently. Your calculated precision should not imply more certainty than your measurements provide.
  • Linearise only when theory supports the transformation. Do not force a straight line because it is easier to analyse.

Graphs

  • Make graphs large and readable, with variable names and units on both axes.
  • Plot uncertainty/error bars where appropriate.
  • Use a best-fit line or model supported by the physics.
  • Use all relevant data when finding a gradient — never only the first and last points.
  • Where appropriate, determine gradient/intercept uncertainty using steepest and shallowest acceptable lines or another justified method.
Watch your language: “linear” does not automatically mean “directly proportional”. A directly proportional relationship also requires consistency with a zero intercept within uncertainty.

6 marks

Conclusion

Answer the research question and justify the answer using your analysis and accepted scientific context.

A useful four-part structure

  1. Answer the RQ. State the relationship or value your investigation found.
  2. Use evidence. Quote the relevant gradient, fitted relationship, calculated value or trend with uncertainty.
  3. Compare with physics. Explain how the result agrees or disagrees with the theoretical model or accepted value.
  4. Qualify the claim. State what the uncertainty, range or model assumptions mean for how confidently you can answer the RQ.
Do more than describe the graph. “The graph went up” is not a physics conclusion. Explain what the trend means and whether it is consistent with the predicted relationship.

If you compare with a published physical value, use a suitable source and cite it. If an exact accepted value is not available, a justified plausible range may be more appropriate.

6 marks

Evaluation

Identify specific methodological weaknesses or limitations, explain their relative impact and propose realistic improvements.

Use this chain

Evidence → Limitation → Impact → Improvement
PartQuestion to ask yourself
EvidenceWhat in my data, graph, uncertainty or observations shows there is a problem?
LimitationWhat specific feature of my method/model caused it?
ImpactHow would this affect the measured quantity, gradient, intercept, spread or conclusion?
ImprovementWhat realistic change would reduce or test that effect, and why would it help?

Strong

“The y-intercept was above zero even though the model predicts zero. This suggests a systematic offset in … . Measuring … independently and correcting for … would test whether the intercept is caused by … .”

Weak

“There was human error. I would repeat it more and use better equipment.”

One well-supported limitation can be more valuable than a long list of generic errors. Focus on issues that actually matter to your data and conclusion.

Do not write “human error”. Name the actual measurement or procedural limitation and explain its effect.

Across the whole report

Communication

  • Use short sections with useful headings and a logical order.
  • Number and refer to figures, tables, graphs and important equations.
  • Use correct physics terminology, symbols and SI units.
  • Use a descriptive title.
  • Keep only material that helps the reader understand or assess the investigation.
  • The report must remain within the current IB word limit; your school's guidance uses a maximum of 3000 words.
  • A title page and table of contents are not necessary unless your teacher asks for them.
