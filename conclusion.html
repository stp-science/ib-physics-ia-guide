Criterion • 6 marks

Conclusion

Use your analysed data to answer the research question, then connect that answer back to accepted physics. A conclusion is an argument supported by evidence, not a summary of what you did.

Research DesignData AnalysisConclusionEvaluation

The core structure

Build the conclusion in four moves

1AnswerState what relationship or value the investigation found.
2EvidenceUse the gradient, model, calculated value, trend and uncertainty.
3CompareRelate the result to accepted theory or a published value.
4QualifyExplain what uncertainty and model limits mean for the claim.

Step 1

Answer the research question directly

Start with the answer. Do not make the reader hunt through a description of your graph to find it.

Stronger

Across the measured range, tension was proportional to 1/n2, consistent with the standing-wave model at fixed frequency and string length.

Too descriptive

The graph went down as harmonic number increased.

Step 2

Use quantitative evidence

Refer to the processed result that actually answers the question. Depending on the investigation this may be:

  • a gradient with uncertainty;
  • a fitted equation or exponent;
  • a calculated physical constant;
  • a percentage difference from a model;
  • a pattern in residuals;
  • or a trend compared with the size of the error bars.
Use numbers. “The data agree with theory” is much weaker than stating the measured value, its uncertainty and the theoretical or accepted value it is being compared with.

Step 3

Compare with accepted physics

Explain whether your result is consistent with the relevant model or accepted value.

  • State the theoretical prediction clearly.
  • Use a reliable source for any accepted value not already established by the course.
  • Where appropriate, calculate percentage difference.
  • Compare the size of the difference with the uncertainty of your experimental result.
Percentage difference=|xexp−xaccepted|xaccepted×100%

Step 4

Use uncertainty to qualify the conclusion

Do not treat uncertainty as a separate add-on. It affects how confidently you can claim agreement, disagreement or a particular relationship.

Example thinking

If your experimental value is 343 ± 8 m s−1 and an accepted value for the room conditions lies inside that interval, it is reasonable to say the result is consistent with the accepted value within experimental uncertainty.

Physics, not just statistics

Explain what the result means physically

The 2025 feedback used for this guide emphasises that a conclusion should relate clearly to the physics concept, not simply repeat statistical analysis.

Ask:

  • Does the trend match the physical model?
  • Does the gradient represent a meaningful physical quantity?
  • Is the intercept physically sensible?
  • Where does the model begin to deviate from the data?
  • Are those deviations larger than the experimental uncertainty?

If relevant

Address exclusions and unusual points

If an outlier was removed, identify it and justify why. Do not quietly omit inconvenient data. If the point was kept, explain whether it changes the fitted relationship or conclusion.

Writing support

A concise paragraph structure

1. Answer: “The results show that…”

2. Evidence: “The best-fit relationship/gradient/value was … ± …”

3. Theory: “The model predicts … because …”

4. Comparison: “The experimental result differs from the accepted/predicted value by …% and is/is not consistent within uncertainty.”

5. Qualification: “Therefore, over the measured range…”

Do not copy this wording mechanically. The evidence and physics should determine what you actually write.

Assessed exemplar comparison

Why did one Conclusion get 6/6 and another 2/6?

A conclusion needs more than a statement that the hypothesis was supported. It must be justified by the processed data and connected correctly to accepted physics.

6 / 6Harmonic motion oscillations

What the full-mark work did

  • Built the conclusion directly from detailed, precise analysis and uncertainty treatment.
  • Showed that the measured relationship agreed with the known SHM model.
  • Focused on the relationship the investigation was designed to test, rather than forcing an unnecessary numerical comparison.
  • Kept the conclusion fully consistent with the processed data and graphs.

Actual student work - short extract

“After having collected and processed the data, it can be concluded that a change in the mass of an oscillating object indeed changes the period as described by the known equation. This establishes the validity of the equation for the limited range of my experiment.”

The claim is tied to the known model and is deliberately limited to the range actually investigated.

Assessor emphasis: the conclusion was sound, justified and fully aligned with accepted theory.

2 / 6Hydrodynamics of a water rocket

What limited the mark

  • The qualitative prediction was supported, but the chosen curve of best fit was not physically justified.
  • The meaning of the y-intercept was not considered properly.
  • The conclusion did not explain a defensible model for the changing pressure, changing mass and complex rocket motion.
  • Repeated references to Newton's laws and momentum did not count as a relevant comparison with accepted scientific context.

Actual student work - short extract

“My original hypothesis of a logarithmic function was proven to be true. The physical meaning of this equation is obvious: the greater the pressure the greater the range.”

The student treats a fitted curve as proof. The assessor points out that many equations could fit a small dataset, so the form of the relationship still needs physical justification.

Assessor emphasis: a result can look plausible but still be weak if the mathematical fit and physical model are not justified.

What should you learn from this?

Do not finish with “my hypothesis was correct.” State the quantitative result, explain what the graph or fitted parameter means physically, use uncertainty, and compare with the specific theory that actually models your system.
