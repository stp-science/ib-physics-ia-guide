Research Design

Turn an interesting idea into a focused, measurable and reproducible investigation. This section is about making good experimental decisions before the final data collection begins.

Research DesignData AnalysisConclusionEvaluation

The goal

What a strong Research Design does

A strong design makes the purpose and method of the investigation clear enough that another student could understand what is being tested and reproduce the investigation.

Focused question

One clear independent variable and one measurable dependent variable in a specific physical system.

Relevant physics

Explain only the theory needed to predict or interpret the relationship. Avoid general history or unrelated background.

Justified choices

Explain why the range, equipment, controls and measurement method are suitable.

Reproducible method

Give enough detail for another student to repeat what you actually did.

Step 1

Write a strong research question

How does [independent variable] affect [dependent variable] in [specific physical system]?

Your question should be narrow enough to investigate properly but not so narrow that it becomes a one-value demonstration.

Stronger

How does initial angular displacement affect the percentage deviation of a pendulum's measured period from the small-angle model?

Too vague

How does a pendulum work?

Avoid two independent variables. The 2025 feedback used for this guide notes that poorly defined investigations, especially those with two independent variables, often struggled.

Step 2

Explain the physics you actually need

Your background theory should help the reader understand what relationship you expect and why.

  • Define the important physical quantities and symbols.
  • State the equation or model that links your variables.
  • Explain the predicted relationship: linear, inverse, square-root, constant, and so on.
  • State important assumptions that the model relies on.
  • Reference equations or information that are not standard course material.
Do not over-derive. Standard textbook equations do not need pages of algebra. Explain what the equation means and how it applies to your investigation.

Step 3

Identify and control the right variables

VariableWhat to explain
IndependentWhat you deliberately change, the range you use and why that range is suitable.
DependentWhat you measure or calculate, how it is measured and with what precision.
ControlledPhysical quantities that could affect the relationship and therefore need to be kept constant.
Controlled variables are not a list of equipment. Focus on physical quantities that could change the result. Accuracy-related actions belong in the method.

Step 4

Choose a useful range and enough values

Your range should be wide enough to reveal the relationship but still measurable with your equipment.

  • For a simple linear relationship, the supplied school guidance uses at least five independent-variable values as a minimum.
  • Use more points for a non-linear relationship or where you expect a model to begin breaking down.
  • Space values sensibly across the range rather than clustering them in one region.
  • Avoid values where the apparatus becomes unsafe or the dependent change is too small to measure reliably.

Step 5

Pilot before you collect the final dataset

A pilot is a short test of the method. It is one of the best opportunities to improve the IA before time is lost collecting weak data.

✓
Can I measure the dependent variable with enough precision?
✓
Does changing the IV produce a measurable effect across my range?
✓
Can I collect enough repeats in the available time?
✓
Are any control variables changing unexpectedly?
✓
Do I know what graph I expect to make from the final data?

Step 6

Choose and describe equipment properly

Do more than list apparatus. Explain the measurement decisions that matter.

  • Give instrument resolution or measurement uncertainty where relevant.
  • Explain why a light gate, motion sensor, video method, ruler or other instrument is suitable.
  • If software or a data logger is used, explain what it measures and how the value is obtained.
  • Include a labelled diagram or clear photograph when it makes the setup easier to reproduce.

Step 7

Write a reproducible method

The method can be numbered steps or concise paragraphs. It should describe what you actually did, not a generic textbook procedure.

  1. State how the apparatus is set up and which dimensions/conditions are fixed.
  2. Explain how the independent variable is set and measured.
  3. Explain exactly how the dependent variable is measured.
  4. State how many repeats are made and how repeat values are handled.
  5. Explain how you move to the next IV value while maintaining important controls.
  6. Include any calibration, alignment or zeroing that is important to the measurement.
Do not write 45 tiny steps. Enough detail to reproduce the investigation is good; unnecessary detail makes the report harder to follow.

Final design check

Safety and other considerations

Address genuine risks briefly and specifically. Do not invent hazards to make the section look longer.

Examples include laser eye safety, falling masses, unstable stands, electrical equipment near water, hot equipment or glassware. If there is no meaningful risk beyond normal laboratory practice, say so briefly.
