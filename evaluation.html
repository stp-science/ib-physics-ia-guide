Criterion • 6 marks

Evaluation

Use evidence from your own data and method to identify the limitations that matter most, explain their effect and propose realistic improvements.

Research DesignData AnalysisConclusionEvaluation

The core idea

Use the evidence chain

EvidenceWhat in the data or observations shows a problem?
→
LimitationWhat specific feature of the method or model caused it?
→
ImpactHow did it affect the measured result, graph or conclusion?
→
ImprovementWhat realistic change would reduce or test the effect?

Step 1

Find limitations from evidence

Start with what you can see in the investigation rather than a generic list of possible errors.

  • A non-zero intercept when theory predicts zero.
  • Error bars that become much larger in part of the range.
  • Residuals showing a curve rather than random scatter.
  • Repeated measurements with unusually large spread.
  • A measurement that was difficult to define consistently, such as judging resonance or rebound height.
  • A control variable that drifted during the investigation.
  • A clear physical effect neglected by the model, such as friction, damping or air resistance.

Step 2

Name the limitation precisely

Specific

“At the lower frequencies the resonance peak was broad, so the resonant air-column length could only be located to about ±5 mm.”

Generic

“There was human error when measuring.”

Do not use “human error”. It does not identify what went wrong or how it affected the result.

Step 3

Explain the impact

This is where many evaluations become too vague. Say what the limitation would actually do to the data.

Instead of…Explain…
“This reduced accuracy.”Whether it increases random spread, shifts all values systematically, changes the gradient, changes the intercept or affects only one part of the range.
“Friction affected the result.”Whether friction makes measured acceleration systematically lower than the frictionless prediction and whether its relative importance changes with angle.
“It was hard to measure.”Why the uncertainty became large and how that weakened the ability to distinguish between competing models.

Step 4

Propose a realistic improvement

The improvement should directly address the limitation you just identified.

Strong improvement

Replace manual timing with video analysis at a known frame rate, allowing the period to be determined from frame number and reducing reaction-time uncertainty.

Weak improvement

Use better equipment.

Explain why the improvement would reduce the limitation. If an improvement introduces a new limitation, acknowledge that too.

Quality over quantity

Prioritise the limitations that matter

The 2025 feedback used for this guide notes that one insightful evaluation of a major issue is better than several vague comments. Focus first on limitations that genuinely influence the conclusion.

High priority

Large effect on gradient/value, systematic offset, model breakdown, or uncertainty comparable with the observed effect.

Lower priority

Tiny effects that are much smaller than the measurement uncertainty or generic possibilities not supported by evidence.

Useful distinction

Random and systematic effects

RandomSystematic
Causes scatter between repeated measurements.Shifts measurements consistently in one direction or changes the model relationship.
Often visible as spread/error bars.May appear as a non-zero intercept, consistent offset or biased gradient.
Repeats can help estimate its size.Repeating the same biased method does not necessarily remove it.

Worked thinking

From weak to strong evaluation

Example: trolley on a ramp
Evidence: measured acceleration was below g sin θ for every angle, with the largest percentage difference at small angles.

Limitation: rolling resistance is not included in the frictionless model.

Impact: the resisting force reduces net downslope force, creating a systematic low bias. Its effect is proportionally larger when the downslope component of weight is small.

Improvement: independently estimate the resisting force by measuring the minimum incline angle at which the trolley begins to move steadily, or use a lower-friction dynamics track and compare the two datasets.
Example: resonance tube
Evidence: repeated resonant lengths varied more at low frequencies.

Limitation: the resonance maximum was broad and difficult to locate precisely.

Impact: this increases uncertainty in both resonant lengths and therefore in ΔL, producing larger vertical error bars at low frequency.

Improvement: measure sound amplitude with a microphone/sound-level sensor and identify the resonance frequency or length from the peak rather than judging loudness by ear.
Example: bouncing ball
Evidence: rebound height showed greater spread at higher drop heights.

Limitation: small variations in spin and impact orientation caused changes in the rebound trajectory.

Impact: the measured vertical rebound height becomes more variable, increasing uncertainty in the calculated coefficient of restitution.

Improvement: use a mechanical release guide that prevents spin while allowing the ball to fall freely, then test whether the spread decreases.

If useful

Suggest a meaningful next step

A good extension follows naturally from your results. It should not be a completely unrelated experiment.

For example: extend the pendulum investigation to larger amplitudes and compare with a higher-order theoretical correction; or repeat the resonance investigation at different temperatures to test the temperature dependence of the speed of sound.

Assessed exemplar comparison

Why did one Evaluation get 6/6 and another 2/6?

The lower-mark investigation was strong in other criteria, which makes this comparison particularly useful. Evaluation is not a list of possible errors: it is an explanation of specific limitations, their impact and targeted improvements.

6 / 6Harmonic motion oscillations

What the full-mark work did

  • Distinguished and discussed random and systematic effects.
  • Used evidence from the graph, including a small systematic shift, to identify possible causes.
  • Considered assumptions in the theoretical model rather than only procedural mistakes.
  • Explained focused improvements such as more precise timing and accounting for additional moving mass.
  • Noticed investigation-specific details such as slight spring unwinding and considered the scope of the final equation.

Actual student work - short extract

“After performing my experiment I discovered that in some cases the mass of the spring itself contributes the theoretical value of mass in equation #1... This might well be the systematic shift that is subtle but apparent in my data.”

This starts from evidence in the graph, identifies a specific physical cause and explains the direction of a systematic effect.

Assessor emphasis: the student explained the impact of specific limitations and linked realistic improvements directly to them.

2 / 6Drop and rebound height

What limited the mark

  • Some limitations were mentioned, but they were not explored in enough depth.
  • Comments focused partly on graph quality and a high-speed camera rather than evaluating the overall method and analysis.
  • The limitations of using a two-dimensional simulation were only treated superficially.
  • Improvements were mostly stated rather than explained in relation to a specific limitation and its effect on the result.
  • The student missed opportunities to discuss scale reading, compounded uncertainty and how the simulation differed from real bouncing surfaces.

Actual student work - short extract

“Algodoo simulates two dimensional objects while a real ball drop involves a three dimensional ball. This alone would cause a deviation in the resulting data.”
“First of all I would like to see if the COR remains constant after several bounces...”

The limitation is named, but the mechanism and effect are not explained. The next idea is mainly an extension rather than a direct improvement to the identified limitation.

Assessor emphasis: awareness of problems is not enough; the top band requires explanation of their impact and why the proposed improvement would address them.

What should you learn from this?

Build each evaluation point as Evidence → Limitation → Impact → Improvement. Two or three deeply explained limitations are more useful than a long list of generic errors.
