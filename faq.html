IA FAQ

Short answers to common questions. If your teacher gives different instructions for your class, follow those instructions.

Does my experiment need to be completely original?
No. A manageable experiment carried out carefully can produce a strong IA. What matters is a clear question, valid data, appropriate analysis, a justified conclusion and an evaluation that shows real understanding. Avoid copying a routine textbook verification without adapting the question or analysis.
Do I need a hypothesis?
No. For a Physics IA, a separate hypothesis is not necessary when you are testing a well-established theoretical relationship. Explain the relevant theory and what it predicts instead.
Can I investigate two independent variables?
This usually makes the investigation harder to control and analyse. The supplied 2025 feedback specifically warns that ill-defined investigations with two independent variables struggled. Keep one clear IV unless there is a compelling and approved reason not to.
How many values of my independent variable should I use?
At least five is the supplied minimum for a simple linear relationship, but more is usually better and non-linear relationships need more points. Choose a range wide enough to reveal the behaviour but still measurable with your apparatus.
How many repeats should I do?
Repeat the dependent measurement enough to judge its spread. Around five repeats is a useful target when practical, but the correct number depends on measurement time, variability and the method.
Should I force my graph through the origin?
No. Only use a zero intercept when the theoretical model and your uncertainty justify it. A non-zero intercept can itself be important evidence of a systematic effect or an incomplete model.
Can I remove an outlier?
Only with justification. Check for a clear measurement/procedural reason and, if possible, repeat the condition. State what you removed and why. Do not remove points simply because they make the graph look worse.
What should I say instead of “human error”?
Name the actual problem: reaction-time limitation, parallax, uncertainty locating resonance, variable release technique, friction at a pulley, alignment of a ray, and so on. Then explain how it affects the data.
Do I need a personal engagement section?
No. The current guidance used for this site states that a separate personal-engagement section is no longer required and can distract from the physics if it adds irrelevant material.
How long should my report be?
Keep it focused and within the current IB requirements. The supplied school guidance uses a maximum of 3000 words and suggests that roughly 10–15 pages is often workable, but quality and relevance matter more than filling pages.
Do I need a title page and contents page?
No, not unless your teacher asks for them. Use a descriptive title, page numbers, your required student code and word count, and make graphs/tables easy to read.
Can I use AI while working on the IA?
Follow your teacher's instructions and current IB rules. AI output is not an authoritative scientific source. Never invent data, sources or analysis, and do not submit generated writing as if it were your own work. Use approved tools in ways your teacher has explicitly allowed.
