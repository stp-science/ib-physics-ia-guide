Resources

Short reference material for the parts of the IA that students most often need to check while working.

±

Uncertainty

Keep measured values and uncertainty precision consistent. For repeated measurements, use a justified spread such as half-range or standard deviation, but never report an uncertainty smaller than the meaningful instrument resolution.

↗

Graphs

Choose the graph from the physics model. Linearise only when theory supports it. Label axes with units, use error bars where appropriate and use the full dataset when fitting a gradient.

123

Significant figures

Do not imply unrealistic precision. For sums/differences, decimal-place precision matters; for products/quotients, significant figures matter.

Graph decisions

Before you press “add trendline”

Should my graph go through the origin?
Only if the physics model predicts a zero intercept and your data/uncertainty support that choice. Do not force the intercept to zero simply because the equation looks proportional.
Should I linearise my graph?
Linearisation is useful when it follows directly from the theoretical model and makes the predicted relationship testable. Do not transform data just to manufacture a straight line.
What if one point looks like an outlier?
Do not delete it automatically. Check the raw measurement, repeat that condition if possible, and justify any exclusion using evidence. You can often show the analysis with and without the point.
How many data points do I need?
For a straightforward linear relationship, five independent-variable values is an absolute minimum in the supplied school guidance; use more where practical, especially for non-linear relationships. Repeated dependent measurements improve your estimate of spread.
